# Writing About CPL in the Chancellor's Office Style

The California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office sets the usage for writing about the system in its Communications Style Guide, prepared by the Office of Communications and Marketing and updated in March 2025. The guide builds on the Associated Press Stylebook, and where the two differ, the Chancellor's Office guide governs. Its Brand Basics set the colors, typefaces and logo rules. This page gathers the rules that writing about credit for prior learning (CPL) meets most often. Logo files, fonts and templates come from the Chancellor's Office brand pages, listed at the end, and questions about the guide go to the Office of Communications and Marketing.

## Names

The guide asks writers to use the full name of the office or the system on first reference and a short form after it. It does not use an acronym for either one, even on later references.

| Write | Instead of | Note |
|---|---|---|
| California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, then the Chancellor's Office | CCCCO, system office | Always capitalized. |
| California Community Colleges, then the system or the community college system | CCC, California Community College System | The name takes a singular verb: the California Community Colleges is. |
| the California community colleges, California's community colleges | California Community Colleges (for the colleges as a group) | Lowercase when the sentence means the colleges rather than the system. |
| Mendocino College, College of the Sequoias | Mendocino, Sequoia College | A college takes its full official name. |
| Chancellor Sonya Christian; the chancellor | the Chancellor (alone) | A title is capitalized only before a name. |
| Board of Governors | board of governors | Capitalized in all cases. |
| Educational Services and Support (ESS), then ESS | ESS on first reference | A division takes its full name first, with the acronym in parentheses. |

A program or initiative is capitalized as part of its formal name, as in the CPL Initiative, and lowercase when it stands alone, as in the initiative. The name of an outside organization follows that organization's own official title.

## Numbers, dates and money

| Topic | The guide's form |
|---|---|
| Numbers | Spell out one through nine and use figures from 10. Spell out a number that opens a sentence. |
| Percentages | Figures and the percent sign with no space: 3.1%. |
| Money | Figures with a dollar sign, and million or billion spelled out: $1.7 million. |
| Academic and fiscal years | The year, a hyphen and the last two digits: 2026-27. A span written as a noun uses to: from 2026 to 2028. |
| Dates | Figures without ordinals. Abbreviate Jan., Feb., Aug., Sept., Oct., Nov. and Dec. when a day follows; spell the month out alone or with a year only: Sept. 30, 2026; September 2026. |
| Times | 3 p.m. and 7:30 p.m.; noon and midnight in place of 12 p.m. and 12 a.m. |
| Telephone numbers | Area code with hyphens. For content read mainly online, (XXX) XXX-XXXX gives a screen reader a natural pause. |

## Punctuation and word forms

| Topic | The guide's form |
|---|---|
| Serial comma | The guide permits it across all agency communications. Use it, and use it the same way throughout a piece. |
| Em dash | A single space on each side: faculty review the training — its content, its assessment and its hours — before a college awards credit. |
| Prefix non | No hyphen before a common word: noncredit, nonscholarship. |
| The suffix wide | One word: statewide, systemwide, campuswide. |
| Acronyms | Avoid them where possible. Spell out the first reference with the acronym in parentheses: credit for prior learning (CPL), full-time equivalent students (FTES). |
| Headlines | Title case, with articles and short prepositions lowercase. No all-capital headlines. |
| Degrees | Spell them out: associate degree, bachelor's degree. No degree after an abbreviation. |
| Word choices | adviser; fundraising; email; online; homepage; login as a noun and log in as a verb. |
| Course names | In quotation marks in running text. |
| Web addresses | The shortest form, without http:// or a trailing slash: cccco.edu. |
| File links | The file type in lowercase with a period: Funding explainer (.pdf). |

## Inclusive language

The guide asks writers to recast a generic he into the plural and to use they for a person who does not specify. It lists gender-neutral terms such as chair, workforce and firefighter. For disability, it uses identity-first language for groups and wherever a person's preference is unknown, prefers accessible parking, and asks writers to avoid afflicted with, battling, suffers from and special needs. It capitalizes Black, Indigenous, Latino and Native American and lowercases white. It accepts people of color in broad references, never for one person, and asks writers to avoid the shorthand POC, BIPOC and BAME outside a direct quotation.

## Typefaces and the logo

The Brand Basics name Crimson Text as the primary typeface, a serif for display and headings, and Source Sans Pro as the secondary, a sans-serif for display and text; no other serif or sans-serif replaces them. The horizontal logo is preferred, with the stacked version for tight spaces, and the clear space around it equals the height of its capital C. The logo is never recolored outside the approved combinations, rearranged, distorted or paired with added words; program lockups come from the Office of Communications and Marketing. The seal serves formal uses such as Board of Governors resolutions, official stationery and diplomas, and is never altered or recreated. Education Code section 71025 protects the name and the mark, and only officials of the Chancellor's Office may use them without written permission from the communications director.
