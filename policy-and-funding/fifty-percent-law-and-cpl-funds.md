# The Fifty Percent Law and Credit for Prior Learning Funds

Colleges that implement credit for prior learning (CPL) pay for work that rarely happens in a classroom: a CPL coordinator, the counseling that precedes the transcription of CPL, classified staff who evaluate military and industry transcripts, and faculty who review credit recommendations against their courses. Business officers ask whether those costs count against the Fifty Percent Law, and the concern is well founded. In 2024-25, 35 of the system's 73 districts reported between 50% and 51% (California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, Fifty Percent Law report, 2026). This note sets out what the statute, the regulations, and the Chancellor's Office accounting manuals say about that work.

## What the Law Requires

Education Code section 84362(d) requires each community college district to expend 50% of its current expense of education on salaries of classroom instructors. Section 84362(a) defines those salaries as the salary of each instructor whose duties are devoted to the instruction of students, the portion of a mixed assignment "actually devoted by the instructor to teaching students of the district," the salaries of instructional aides who work under an instructor's supervision, and the instructors' health and welfare benefits. Title 5, section 59204(a), restates the definition as "that portion of salaries paid for purposes of instruction of students" and includes the benefits of instructors and aides.

The statute's definition of an instructor, in section 84362(b)(1), refers to an employee who teaches "for at least one full instructional period each schoolday." The Chancellor's Office's accounting guidance directs districts to count the teaching portion of any assignment and to prorate mixed assignments between instructional and noninstructional salary.

## Where CPL Work Falls

Most CPL work sits outside that definition. The Budget and Accounting Manual classifies counselors' salaries as noninstructional (Object 1200), places counseling in activity 6300 and transcript evaluation in activity 6200, and states that "any release time or additional stipends paid for coordinators, supervisors, and departmental chairs are noninstructional." It records faculty stipends paid for grants, training, and similar work as noninstructional (Object 1400). The Chancellor's Office's annual reports list among the excluded costs the "salaries and benefits of individuals who provide educational services directly to students, such as librarians and counselors," and "reassigned time for instructional program and curriculum development and modification."

| CPL cost | How the Budget and Accounting Manual classifies it | Counts as instruction |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinator release time or stipend | Noninstructional, Object 1200 | No |
| Counseling, including the CPL step before transcription | Noninstructional, Object 1200, activity 6300 | No |
| Classified staff time to evaluate transcripts and credentials | Admissions and records, activity 6200 | No |
| Faculty stipend to review credit recommendations | Noninstructional, Object 1400 | No |
| Counseling faculty teaching a course | Instructional salary, Objects 1100 and 1300, prorated | Yes, for the teaching time |
| Faculty intermittent committee duties to evaluate or improve the educational program | Instructional salary, Object 1100 | Yes, when charged to an instructional activity; districts confirm with their auditors |

## Restricted Funds Sit Outside the Calculation

The calculation divides salaries of classroom instructors by the current expense of education, and the law removes restricted funding from that base. Section 84362(c) excludes "the amount expended from categorical aid received from the federal or state government which funds were granted for expenditures in a program not incurring any instructor salary expenditures or requiring disbursement of the funds without regard to the requirements of this section." Title 5, section 59204(b), carries the same exclusions and adds "other monies received which are restricted by an external party, law or other legal requirement."

The Chancellor's Office applies the rule the same way in its manuals. The Budget and Accounting Manual defines the current expense of education as "the Unrestricted General Fund expenditures of a community college district," and section 421 of the Contracted District Audit Manual directs auditors to test a sample of state and federal categorical program expenditures to confirm they were excluded. The Chancellor's Office's Fifty Percent Law page advises districts: "If a grant has the flexibility, charge appropriate non-instructional costs to the grant in order to move these expenditures to the restricted fund."

A cost paid from restricted funds therefore enters neither side of the calculation. The same cost paid from unrestricted funds enlarges the base alone and lowers the percentage.

| Illustration: a district adds $200,000 of CPL coordination and counseling | Classroom instructor salaries | Current expense of education | Percentage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before the addition | $50.6 million | $100.0 million | 50.60% |
| Paid from restricted funds | $50.6 million | $100.0 million | 50.60% |
| Paid from unrestricted funds | $50.6 million | $100.2 million | 50.50% |

The figures are illustrative and describe no actual district.

## CPL Implementation Funds

Education Code section 78093.2(d)(1), added in 2026, directs the Chancellor's Office to "allocate designated funds to support implementation of this article at each campus." The 2025-26 implementation grants of $50,000 per college, set out in memo ESS 25-82, carry stated commitments and allowable uses that include "increased coordination, student engagement, and counseling efforts designed to expand CPL." The Budget and Accounting Manual reserves its restricted subfund for resources "specifically restricted by laws, regulations, donors, or other outside agencies as to their expenditure."

Read together, these sources support recording CPL implementation funds as restricted, which keeps the coordination, counseling, and evaluation they pay for outside the Fifty Percent Law calculation. ESS 25-82 does not address accounting classification, so each district's business office makes that determination with its auditor.

## CPL Work That Counts as Instruction

Districts that pay for CPL work from unrestricted funds have two practices the Chancellor's Office already recognizes as instruction. The first is teaching. The Fifty Percent Law page tells districts to "code ALL instruction to instructional salaries," including "non-instructional faculty, like counselors and librarians who teach." Several colleges teach a one-unit portfolio development course in which students assemble evidence of their prior learning, among them San Joaquin Delta College ("Portfolio Development," COUN 70) and West Hills College Coalinga ("Portfolio Development," IS-051). A counselor's teaching time in such a course counts as instructional salary, and its enrollments generate full-time equivalent students (FTES).

The second is curriculum work paid as intermittent duty. The same page tells districts to code as instructional "intermittent duties…in connection with committee work…whose purpose is the evaluation or improvement of the educational program," with curriculum development as its example. Faculty review of credit recommendations against course outlines fits that description when the district pays it as intermittent duty and charges it to the discipline's instructional activity. Reassigned time and coordinator stipends remain noninstructional, and the Chancellor's Office's training materials advise districts to review curriculum work with their auditors.

## An Open Question

No source reviewed for this note addresses whether faculty assessment of prior learning counts as instruction of students under section 84362(a). Title 5, section 55050, reserves that assessment to faculty in the discipline who normally teach the course, and the faculty member determines the credit. The statute speaks of the "instruction of students" in one clause and of "teaching students" in the next, and the answer may turn on which reading applies.

## Policy Context

The State Auditor's report 2023-126 (April 2025) found oversight of the law ineffective and recommended that the Legislature consider counting the salaries and benefits of counselors and librarians toward the requirement. Earlier proposals to count counselors, among them Assembly Bill 806 (2013), did not advance, and faculty organizations have supported counting counselors only if the required percentage rises with them. In September 2026, EdSource reported that the Governor signed Assembly Bill 2121, which for five years excludes from the calculation the unrestricted funds districts use to replace terminated federal Minority-Serving Institution and TRIO grants.
