## Executive Summary

The California Community Colleges Credit for Prior Learning (CPL) Initiative has reached a critical inflection point in its mission to expand equitable access to college credit for working adults, veterans, and apprentices across the state. As of July 2, 2026, the initiative has served 48,286 Californians with CPL awards—nearly one-fifth of the 250,000-student target by 2030—while transcripting over 100,000 units of college credit and generating an estimated $322 million in tuition savings for students. These outcomes directly advance the policy objectives established under AB 1071 and align with the Vision 2030 strategic framework, particularly Action 1a (removing barriers to student success) and Action 5 (accelerating completion through innovation).

The fiscal and educational returns on state investment are substantial and measurable. With 100 of 116 system colleges now actively participating in CPL implementation, the initiative has created a scalable infrastructure supported by the statewide Military Articulation Platform (MAP), faculty-driven credit recommendation workgroups, and AI-enhanced tools that reduce administrative burden while maintaining academic rigor. The Veteran Sprint alone has processed 24,939 Joint Services Transcripts representing 114,000 eligible units valued at $160 million in avoided tuition costs, while 89 colleges now award credit for military basic training—a six-fold increase from baseline. Beacon Economics projects a 20-year cumulative impact of $1.3 billion in student savings and accelerated workforce entry, positioning CPL as a high-leverage investment in both human capital development and regional economic competitiveness.

The initiative's success in exceeding several 2025-26 targets—including transcripted units, institutional participation, and working adult enrollment—demonstrates both strong demand and effective implementation across diverse college contexts. Strategic investments in AI-assisted credential matching, apprenticeship pathways, and statewide credit recommendations have built a replicable model now being adopted nationwide. However, sustained progress toward the 2030 goals requires continued legislative support, including the proposed $2 million in additional ongoing funding and $35 million one-time appropriation to expand infrastructure, deepen industry partnerships, and ensure all 116 colleges achieve full implementation capacity.

## Key Metrics & Progress

The initiative has served 48,286 unique students cumulatively, including 24,939 from military populations, 23,424 working adults and other learners, and 754 apprentices and journey workers. This represents 19 percent attainment of the 250,000-student goal by 2030 and positions the system ahead of the linear growth trajectory required to meet legislatively aligned targets.

Transcripted units have surpassed the annual goal of 85,000, reaching 100,449 units as of July 2026. This milestone reflects improved data reporting, enhanced registrar training, and streamlined transcript processing across participating colleges. The breakdown includes 14,000 military units, 86,000 workforce and other units, and 6,000 apprentice units. Applied credits—representing units accepted toward degree or certificate requirements—total 230,673, demonstrating that CPL is not merely ceremonial but functionally accelerates students toward credential completion.

Institutional participation metrics reveal broad system engagement. All 100 of 116 colleges are now classified as active participants, including 15 designated as "Leading Colleges" demonstrating advanced implementation and 85 as "Advancing Colleges" showing measurable progress. An additional 114 colleges have received $50,000 implementation grants from the $15 million one-time appropriation authorized under AB 123, ensuring resource equity across large urban districts and small rural campuses alike. The MAP platform now hosts 2,656 credit recommendation exhibits created by 88 originating colleges, with 132 statewide collaborative standards adopted by 61 colleges—creating transferable pathways that reduce duplication and faculty workload.

Economic impact estimates reinforce the initiative's return on investment. Beacon Economics calculates $322 million in cumulative student savings based on eligible units at current enrollment costs, with a 20-year projection of $1.3 billion when accounting for accelerated completion, reduced time-to-degree, and earlier workforce entry. For context, the state's cumulative investment in CPL infrastructure since inception totals approximately $24 million, yielding a current return ratio of 13:1 in direct student savings alone—before accounting for broader economic multipliers associated with workforce participation and tax revenue.

Faculty-led credit recommendations—the academic foundation of the initiative—have reached 12,617 systemwide, including 1,297 collaborative recommendations vetted through 11 discipline-specific workgroups spanning high-demand sectors such as welding, HVAC, nursing, emergency medical services, and advanced manufacturing. These recommendations cover military occupational specialties, industry certifications, apprenticeship completions, and professional licenses, creating transparent and equitable pathways validated by subject matter experts and aligned with labor market priorities identified in regional economic forecasting.

## Project Highlights

### Activity 1: AI-Enhanced CPL Infrastructure

The Military Articulation Platform (MAP) serves as the digital backbone of California's CPL system, and its evolution during the 2025-26 year reflects substantial progress in user experience, technical capacity, and institutional interoperability. Project 1.1 has advanced eight major feature areas through the completion of over 50 development tickets, including the upcoming production release of the Student CPL Portal in May 2026 and enhanced AI-assisted Joint Services Transcript parsing that reduces processing time from hours to minutes while maintaining accuracy standards. Accessibility improvements ensure compliance with state and federal standards, expanding usability for students with disabilities. The platform now serves as a centralized repository for credit recommendations, transcript processing workflows, and student self-service tools, reducing administrative fragmentation and enabling real-time progress tracking for both students and institutions.

System integration and interoperability work under Project 1.2 has established foundational connections with external credentialing and student information systems. The integration with Credential Engine enables competency-level alignment using the Credential Transparency Description Language (CTDL), allowing California to participate in national credential registry standards while maintaining state-specific academic requirements. Phase 1 integration with the Program Pathways Mapper is complete, creating linkages between CPL awards and degree planning tools that help students visualize how prior learning applies to their educational goals. Planned integrations with the CVC Course Exchange and Career Passport will extend these capabilities, ensuring CPL data flows seamlessly across the systems students use to plan, enroll, and document their educational and career progress.

The CPL Student Portal under Project 1.3 represents a paradigm shift in student access to prior learning evaluation. Currently in final development, the portal consolidates previously fragmented processes—credential upload, military transcript requests, credit recommendation search, and petition submission—into a single AI-enabled interface accessible through standard CCCApply authentication. Phase 1 features include a comprehensive landing page, streamlined JST upload tools, and intelligent matching algorithms that suggest relevant credit opportunities based on uploaded credentials. Phase 2 enhancements scheduled for December 2026 will integrate Program Pathways Mapper functionality, CVC course search capabilities, and an AI-assisted resume builder that helps students articulate verified skills to employers, creating a continuum from prior learning recognition to career advancement.

The California Credential Registry under Project 1.4 remains in its foundational year, with no credentials yet published but significant infrastructure development underway. The registry will catalog industry certifications, licenses, and apprenticeship completions that have received faculty-validated credit recommendations, creating transparency for students, employers, and educational institutions about which credentials carry transfer value within the CCC system. Development of a standardized credential data schema incorporating Taxonomy of Programs (TOP) and Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) codes ensures alignment with existing student information systems and labor market data frameworks. While full public launch is not targeted until 2026-27, the groundwork being laid will enable California to establish one of the nation's most comprehensive state-level credential registries with direct linkage to academic credit pathways.

AI certification-to-course matching tools advanced under Project 5.2 have completed Phase 1 coverage of 117 industry certifications across priority sectors including information technology, healthcare, advanced manufacturing, and logistics. These AI-assisted recommendations provide faculty with initial course equivalency suggestions based on learning outcomes analysis, competency frameworks, and labor market validation, substantially reducing the time required to evaluate new credentials while maintaining faculty governance over final credit decisions. Phase 2 beta testing is expanding the certification pool and refining machine learning algorithms based on faculty feedback, with production release planned for June 2026. This capability positions California to respond rapidly to emerging industry credentials without requiring months-long faculty workgroup formation for each new certification.

### Activity 2: Faculty Workgroups & Credit Recommendations

Faculty-led credit recommendation development remains the academic cornerstone of the CPL Initiative's legitimacy and scalability. Project 2.1 has generated 288 cumulative statewide credit recommendations through the Academic Senate for California Community Colleges (ASCCC) Pathways to Credit workgroups, with 281 adopted by colleges during the current academic year and 230 currently progressing through institutional review and approval workflows. These recommendations span military occupational specialties, apprenticeship competencies, industry certifications, and professional licenses, covering fields with documented labor shortages and high wage potential. The most recent welding workgroup evaluated over 40 certification programs and published detailed credit recommendations now available for systemwide adoption, while the HVAC workgroup is completing its evaluation cycle with results expected summer 2026.

Project 2.2 has convened 11 of the 25 planned discipline-specific faculty workgroups, exceeding the 2025-26 stretch target of 10 active workgroups. Each workgroup comprises subject matter experts from multiple colleges, industry practitioners, and labor representatives who review learning outcomes, competency standards, assessment methods, and industry recognition criteria to determine appropriate credit values and course equivalencies. The 101 credential exhibits documented through these workgroups create reusable evaluation frameworks that individual colleges can adopt wholesale or adapt to local curriculum, dramatically reducing redundant faculty effort while ensuring academic rigor and consistency. The initiative's goal of 40 active discipline workgroups by 2030 will provide coverage across all major career technical education sectors and selected transfer disciplines.

Common Course Crosswalk development under Project 2.3 is advancing through three parallel methodologies. The electrical and carpentry apprenticeship pilots are developing crosswalks through traditional faculty collaboration, while an AI-assisted approach uses machine learning to generate preliminary crosswalks that faculty review and validate. This hybrid model balances efficiency with academic governance, allowing rapid expansion while maintaining faculty authority over curricular decisions. The Common Course Crosswalk functionality will be integrated into the revised MAP Exhibit module launching April 2026, enabling colleges to share validated equivalencies and reducing the need for each institution to independently evaluate the same credentials. This shared infrastructure is particularly valuable for small colleges with limited faculty resources in specialized technical fields.

### Activity 3: CPL Data Infrastructure

The initiative has exceeded its ambitious 2025-26 target for student participation, serving 43,630 cumulative Californians with CPL awards across all populations. Project 3.1 demonstrates particularly strong growth in working adult populations (21,552 students) and veterans/service members (22,149 students), both exceeding annual targets. The apprentice cohort, while smaller in absolute numbers at 700 students, has exceeded its stretch goal and represents early success in a historically underserved population. These participation rates reflect both increased institutional capacity and improved student awareness, supported by the 25 training events and regional workshops conducted under Project 4.3 that reached over 1,000 faculty, staff, and administrators across the system.

Transcription accuracy and timeliness have improved substantially through enhanced registrar training and standardized data entry protocols developed in collaboration with the Chancellor's Office Management Information Systems unit. Project 3.2 reports 96,449 transcripted CPL units, exceeding the stretch target of 90,000 and representing a significant improvement in data quality from prior years when informal CPL awards were often poorly documented in student records. This improvement enables more accurate completion tracking, financial aid compliance, and research on CPL's impact on student outcomes. The breakdown across populations—12,000 military units, 82,000 workforce units, and 6,000 apprentice units—provides granular data for targeted program improvement and resource allocation.

Institutional participation metrics under Project 3.3 reveal substantial system engagement. The 84 colleges meeting the active participation threshold (10 or more students served and 5 or more articulated credit recommendations) significantly exceeds the stretch target of 100 colleges and demonstrates that CPL has moved from pilot phase to standard practice across diverse institutional contexts. The distribution of $50,000 implementation grants to 114 colleges ensures even small rural campuses have resources to train staff, develop local credit recommendations, and integrate CPL into student onboarding processes. The designation of 15 Leading Colleges provides peer models for the 85 Advancing Colleges, creating communities of practice and mentorship relationships that accelerate learning and problem-solving across the system.

Student impact assessment infrastructure is being developed under Project 3.4, though systematic data collection has not yet begun. The student impact survey tool will be embedded in the Student Portal launching May 2026, enabling the first-ever comprehensive collection of data on how CPL affects degree completion rates, time-to-credential, transfer rates, employment outcomes, and wage progression. This longitudinal data will be essential for demonstrating return on investment to legislators and guiding continuous improvement efforts. The 32 qualitative student stories published under Project 3.5 provide compelling narrative evidence of CPL's transformative potential, with veterans describing how military credit recognition reduced degree completion time by one to two years and working adults explaining how certification credit enabled career transitions without employment interruption.

### Activity 4: Sprints, Projects & Partnerships

The Veteran Sprint under Project 4.1.1 has emerged as the initiative's flagship demonstration of rapid, coordinated implementation at scale. With 24,939 Joint Services Transcripts uploaded—representing 62 percent of the estimated 34,000 enrolled CCC veterans—the sprint has documented 114,000 eligible units valued at $160 million in avoided tuition costs. The sprint's three-part strategy has driven measurable institutional change: 78 of 100 targeted colleges have uploaded five or more JSTs, 89 colleges now award credit for military basic training (compared to fewer than 15 colleges at baseline), and 58 colleges have articulated credit recommendations for American Council on Education (ACE) evaluations covering 679 unique military occupational specialties. This coordinated effort has transformed veteran CPL from an ad hoc practice dependent on individual counselor knowledge to a standardized, transparent system accessible at virtually every CCC campus.

The Apprenticeship Sprint under Project 4.1.2 is building partnerships with the California Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS) and trade organizations to document and recognize apprenticeship completion as college credit. The initiative has documented 678 apprentice students with 6,000 CPL units, establishing baseline data for a population historically disconnected from higher education despite extensive competency-based training. The partnership with Cal-JAC, representing 14,000 first responder apprentices in fire service and emergency medical fields, demonstrates the potential for scaled adoption. The anticipated integration of DAS data covering 30,000+ registered apprentices will create unprecedented visibility into apprenticeship pathways and enable proactive outreach to individuals who have completed journey worker status but lack formal degree credentials.

Strategic partnerships under Project 4.2 have expanded to 20 active collaborations spanning state agencies, industry associations, labor organizations, and educational segments. Key partnerships include the Division of Apprenticeship Standards for apprenticeship data integration, the California Department of Education for career technical education alignment, Credential Engine for national credential registry standards, and WestEd for skills taxonomy development and strategic planning. Emerging partnerships with Futuro Health (healthcare workforce development), the Iron Workers union, and the Public Works Alliance EMS Corps represent targeted sector strategies in high-growth fields. These partnerships provide subject matter expertise, industry validation, student pipeline access, and political support that extend the initiative's reach beyond the CCC system's internal capacity.

Technical assistance and training delivery under Project 4.3 has exceeded stretch targets with 25 training events reaching over 1,000 participants, including the 6th Annual CPL Summit and four regional training workshops tailored to local implementation contexts. These professional development opportunities address the full spectrum of CPL practice—from registrar transcript procedures to counselor student advising techniques to faculty credit recommendation evaluation. The training strategy deliberately targets newly funded colleges showing initial implementation challenges, providing hands-on assistance with MAP platform navigation, policy interpretation, and student communication. Ongoing office hours and individual technical assistance complement formal training events, creating a continuous improvement culture supported by peer learning and documented best practices.

Policy advancement efforts under Project 4.4 have achieved significant legislative and regulatory progress. AB 123's enactment secured the CCC system's first ongoing CPL appropriation ($5 million annually) plus $15 million one-time implementation funding, establishing CPL as a permanent system priority rather than a grant-dependent pilot. Title 5 regulatory amendments have advanced to reduce residency requirements and access barriers that previously prevented students from receiving CPL credit in their first enrollment term. The proposed 2026 budget trailer bill language requesting an additional $2 million ongoing and $35 million one-time appropriation reflects the Chancellor's Office assessment that current funding, while foundational, remains insufficient to achieve full-scale implementation across 116 colleges serving 250,000 students by 2030.
